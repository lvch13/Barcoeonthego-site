How we keep our web tools free forever

By Laura, Barcoe On The Go · November 26, 2026

When a web tool is free, it's fair to ask how long that will last. Plenty of tools start free and then add limits, trials or paywalls once people rely on them. We build ours differently, so that staying free is the easy part.

Why free tools usually stop being free

Most web tools cost real money to run every month. They have servers doing the work, databases storing everyone's data, user accounts to manage and security to keep up with. The more people use them, the more they cost. Sooner or later, that bill has to be paid by someone, and it usually ends up being the user.

So we started from a different question: what if a tool didn't need any of that?

1. The work happens on your device

Our tools are static websites. When you open one, your browser downloads a handful of files, and from then on your own phone or computer does the work. When you make a QR code on Freehold QR, it's drawn right there in your browser. There's no server of ours doing it for you.

That keeps our running costs tiny, and it means the tool doesn't slow down or get more expensive as more people use it.

2. No accounts and no database

We don't ask you to sign up, so there's no account system to build, secure or pay for. And because nothing you make is stored on our side, there's no database full of your information either.

3. What you type stays on your device

What you type into our tools stays on your device. We don't upload it, store it or sell it. That's better for your privacy, and it's one less thing for us to run and protect.

4. Small tools that do one job well

We'd rather build a focused tool that does one job properly than a big platform with a free tier. Small tools are quicker to build, easier to keep working and much cheaper to host.

5. Standing on open-source shoulders

We build on excellent open-source libraries, and we credit them on every tool. For example, Freehold QR uses qr-code-styling, jsQR, JSZip and jsPDF. Their authors made it possible for a small team to build something genuinely useful, and we're grateful for that.

So what does it actually cost?

Very little. Each tool needs a domain name, which costs a few dollars a year, and simple hosting for static files, which is often free. The biggest cost is our time. Our free tools are supported by ads, which pay for hosting and our time and let us keep building new tools, alongside the rest of what we do at Barcoe On The Go, including our Flarewell app and our games.

Our promise for every free tool: no sign-up, no trial clock, no features locked behind a paywall, and nothing you make held hostage. If something you make with our tools works today, it keeps working.

Freehold QR is the first of these tools, and more are on the way. You can see them all in the Free Tools section of our site.
