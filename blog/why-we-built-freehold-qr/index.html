Why we built Freehold QR: free tools shouldn't come with a trial clock

By Laura, Barcoe On The Go · November 26, 2026

A lot of things online are called free. Fewer of them stay that way. QR code generators are one of the clearest examples, and that's why Freehold QR was the first free tool we built.

The problem: free until you print

Search for a free QR code generator and you'll find plenty. Many of them make the same kind of code by default. Instead of putting your website address in the pattern, they put in a short link on their own domain. When someone scans it, the phone goes to that company first, and they forward the visitor on to you.

That setup lets them count scans and change where the code points. It also means your code only works while they keep that forwarding running. When a free trial ends or a plan lapses, the forwarding can stop, and every printed copy stops working at the same time.

The people this catches out are usually small businesses and organizers: a restaurant with codes on every table, a shop with a sign in the window, someone who has just ordered 500 business cards. They tend to find out when a customer says the code goes to an error page.

What we wanted instead

A QR code is just a pattern of squares. It can hold your link, your Wi-Fi details or your contact card directly, with nothing in between. That kind of code, a static code, has no account behind it and no clock on it, so there's nothing that can run out.

We wanted a generator that makes that kind of code, makes it look good, and doesn't ask for anything in return. No sign-up, no watermark, no scan limits and no upgrade screen waiting at the download button.

Why “Freehold”

Freehold means owning something outright: no rent, no landlord and no renewal date. That's how we think a QR code should work. Once you've made it, it's yours, and it keeps working for as long as the place it points to does.

What we built

Freehold QR started as a single generator and grew into a small set of tools that all work the same way:

  • A generator for links, Wi-Fi, contact cards, email, texts, phone calls, WhatsApp, map locations, calendar events and plain text, with your own colors, dot styles and logo.
  • A scan check that reads every code back before you download it and warns you if it won't scan.
  • A QR code expiry checker that tells you whether a code you already have goes straight to its destination or through a service that could switch it off.
  • Printable signs for Wi-Fi, menus, review requests and wedding photos, ready to download as a PDF.
  • A bulk generator and a scanner for when you need lots of codes or want to see what one says.

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded, and once the page has loaded it even works offline.

The honest trade-off

Because the code holds your content directly, you can't edit it after it's printed, and we can't count how many times it's scanned. If you need either of those, the simple fix is to point the code at a page you control, like your own website, and change that page whenever you like.

Already printed some codes? Run them through the free QR code expiry checker to see whether they could stop working.
